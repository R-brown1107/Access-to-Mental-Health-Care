Solutions

The first step towards seeking treatment should not be a strenuous task. When someone who is struggling with their mental health seeks out a provider, the process of figuring out what kind of service is needed and where to go can feel like just another barrier to treatment. The AAMC identifies the shortage of mental health professionals as one of the major barriers to care, with more than 129 million people living in federally designated mental health care shortage areas.To combat this, schools, colleges, doctors, and the community at large can aid in making resources more easily found and help in creating a list of options for someone to choose from. Seeking treatment should not be an exercise for someone who already knows what the process is like. If it is more openly talked about and taught to all people, we can help make the first step of finding help easier.

Furthermore, mental health care should be preventative in nature. The NAMI reports that the average delay between the onset of mental illness symptoms and treatment is 11 years. This is an unacceptable window for someone struggling with mental illness to suffer. I do not think we should think of mental health services in the same way we think of other areas of medicine. We do not wait for an illness to run its course or to become easily treatable before we seek treatment, so why should mental illness be any different? I think making early intervention and preventative care the standard for mental health services would be important in encouraging people to seek help before their lives become exponentially more difficult.

Lastly, mental health care should be available to all who need it and should be able to access it easily, no matter where you are or what your financial situation may be. It doesn't do much good to have mental health service options if the individual needing care doesn't have the opportunity to use them. The AAMC found that 30% of adults with mental illness and an unmet need for care reported not receiving treatment because their insurance did not cover mental health services or did not pay enough. Accessibility does not just mean services being in existence, but that they can be reached by individuals with any given circumstance. Increasing accessibility would require a look at funding, insurance, telehealth services, and the overall prevalence of care in the mental health space.
